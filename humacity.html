The operating framework

Every people decision
has a financial dimension.

Humacity is the framework that names it, measures it, and puts it in a language the boardroom already speaks.

The framework
What this means

HR has spent decades treating people decisions as soft. Humacity holds they are not. Every hire, every exit, every culture decision, every case that gets managed or mismanaged — each one has a measurable financial event attached to it. Most organisations simply never wrote it down.


Humacity names four asset classes that live inside every HR function. They are not metrics. They are categories of financial value that HR generates, protects, or destroys — whether it accounts for them or not. Naming them is the first step to measuring them. Measuring them is the first step to owning the conversation.

The HUMANCE framework

Four asset classes.
One financial language.

Asset Class 01
Trust Capital The financial value of trust
Every organisation runs on trust — between leaders and teams, managers and their reports, the brand and the people it is trying to keep. When trust erodes, the financial event that follows is predictable: attrition spikes, productivity drops, recruitment spend climbs. Most organisations experience this and call it a culture problem. Humacity calls it what it is: a capital erosion event.
Cost when ignored: attrition, productivity loss, rehiring expense
Asset Class 02
Culture Yield The return on culture investment
Culture is not a feeling. It is a ratio. The return on every rupee invested in engagement, learning, and team cohesion against the cost of disengagement, absenteeism, and replacement. Most organisations measure culture through annual surveys and net promoter scores. Humacity measures it through its financial yield — what it returns, and what its absence costs.
Cost when ignored: disengagement, absenteeism, innovation gap
Asset Class 03
Signal Intelligence The quality of people data as an asset
The quality of your people data is an asset. Its absence is a liability. Late attrition signals, incomplete performance data, and hiring decisions made without workforce analytics all carry a cost that does not appear on the HR budget — it appears on the P&L. Humacity treats signal quality as a measurable asset class and its deterioration as a quantifiable financial risk.
Cost when ignored: late signals, poor decisions, compliance exposure
Asset Class 04
Belonging Premium The financial value of genuine inclusion
Genuine belonging generates a financial premium. Organisations where people can bring their full capability to work innovate faster, retain longer, and perform above sector benchmarks. Exclusion — whether by design or neglect — writes off a portion of that premium every quarter. Humacity names and measures what is lost, before the exit interview confirms it.
Cost when ignored: capability underuse, attrition, innovation loss
